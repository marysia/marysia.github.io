It's surprisingly easy to make a confusing graph. 

Something clicked for me when I watched [this youtube video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EMW7io4rSI). Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic makes a distinction between two kinds of data visualization: **exploratory** and **explanatory**. 

In my early days as a baby junior data scientist, I'd tinker around with data and build plots in matplotlib. If I had to present something, I wouldn't present it out cold -- I'd clean it up a bit, name the axes, etc. But it wouldn't exactly be *polished*. If you'd asked me to present my findings, I would have shown up with a bunch of plots that required a few minutes of explaining and me pointing franticly at various points in the chart to truly unpack. Barely more legible than just dumping a table on the slide.  

That changed when I watched [this youtube video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EMW7io4rSI). Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic makes a distinction between two kinds of data visualization: **exploratory** and **explanatory**. 

Exploratory visualizations are the typical data analyst/scientists plots, 
Exploratory is for me. It's how I dig through the data and find the thing worth saying. Explanatory is for the person I'm talking to, and its whole job is to carry that one thing across, fast. The chart that helped me find the answer was almost never the chart that should tell it.

So now I make two. One to understand the data, one to share what I understood. The second one usually means taking things away rather than adding them. Fewer lines, less colour, one clear point. Because having the data on the slide was never the same as the point landing.
